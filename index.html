The Story of Refreshment

Coca‑Cola was born on May 8, 1886 in Atlanta, Georgia. Dr. John Stith Pemberton, a pharmacist, created a distinctive caramel‑colored syrup in a three‑legged brass pot. He carried his new concoction to Jacobs' Pharmacy, where it was mixed with carbonated water and sold for 5 cents a glass — just 9 servings a day.

The name "Coca‑Cola" was suggested by Frank M. Robinson, Pemberton's bookkeeper. He thought the two C's would look great in advertising. The first year's sales totaled $50 — but the spirit was already there.

In 1899, the first bottling plants were opened, making Coca‑Cola accessible beyond soda fountains. By the 1920s, it had become a global phenomenon, crossing oceans and cultures. The iconic contour bottle was introduced in 1915 — a design so distinctive it could be recognized in the dark.

Today, Coca‑Cola is more than a beverage. It's a symbol of connection, of shared moments, of joy that transcends borders. From the Atlanta soda fountain to the world's largest brands, the journey has been nothing short of extraordinary.
